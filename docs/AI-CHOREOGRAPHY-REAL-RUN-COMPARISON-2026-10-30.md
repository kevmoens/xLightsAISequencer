# Real-Run Comparison Findings: Reference vs. AI-Generated "Blinding Lights"

**Status:** findings report, not itself a build-plan phase. Written 2026-10-30 after a real end-to-end
`tools/GenerateOnce` run (build-plan Phase 10 §3e verification) was compared against the real,
hand-sequenced reference `.xsq` for the same song. The concrete phases this report's findings led to are
tracked separately as full phase documents — see §8 below.

This compares **effects, layers, timing, and change frequency only** — explicitly not images, video,
Pictures, Faces, or other media-driven effects, per the request that produced this doc. Both files were
parsed with the exact same tool (`XlightsAI.Studio.Pipeline.EffectAlignmentParser`, already used in this
project's own corpus-analysis pipeline), so the two effect lists are directly comparable — same parser, same
`EffectDB`/`ColorPalettes` resolution, no hand-eyeballing of raw XML.

---

## 1. What was compared

| | Reference (human-sequenced) | AI-generated (this run) |
|---|---|---|
| File | `C:\xLights\The Weeknd - Blinding Lights Lite.xsq` | `C:\xLights\generated\Blinding Lights (Phase10-3e-verify).xsq` (temporary filename for this verification; not a new permanent artifact) |
| Song | The Weeknd — Blinding Lights (Lite edit), 3:20 | same song/audio file |
| Generated by | a human sequencer | `AiChoreographyWholeSongGenerator` → `ChoreographyGenerationPipeline`, Azure OpenAI (`gpt-6-luna`), default style preset |
| Total effect instances | 1,727 | 6,193 |
| Distinct models/groups touched | 14 | 11 |
| Distinct effect names used | 22 | 9 |

The AI version has **3.6x more effect instances** but uses **less than half the effect vocabulary**. Those
two facts together are the throughline of every finding below: the AI version is "busier" by raw count, but
structurally flatter and less varied than the reference.

---

## 2. Finding 1 (headline): change frequency does not track the song's own dynamics

Bucketing every effect's own start time into 10-second windows across the whole song:

| Metric | Reference | AI-generated |
|---|---|---|
| Starts/10s, full range | 32 – 149 | 191 – 357 (191 is the last, partial, bucket) |
| Starts/10s, excluding the song's final partial bucket | 32 – 149 | 296 – 357 |
| Ratio (max/min) | **4.7x** | **1.2x** |

The reference's own density swings nearly 5x across the song — quiet stretches (32/10s around 140-150s,
almost certainly a bridge/breakdown) and clear peaks (149/10s around 160-170s, almost certainly the chorus
after a build). This is exactly what architecture's own "built to the song" framing describes: the show
*feels* different in a quiet verse than in a chorus because the rate of visual change itself changes, not
just which effect is playing.

The AI-generated version's density is **essentially flat** — every 10-second window (except the trailing,
naturally-shorter final one) lands within a narrow 296-357 band regardless of section. A listener would
hear a dramatic dynamic shift from this song's intro into its chorus/drop, but would not see one: the light
show's own pace of change barely moves.

This is a real, measurable gap distinct from (and probably more impactful than) which Looks or effects are
assigned — it means the "always-moving"/discrete-cadence machinery in `LookCompiler`/`AlwaysMovingGapSplitter`
is successfully keeping every prop moving, but nothing in the pipeline is currently pushing the *rate* of
that movement up or down with the song's own energy.

## 3. Finding 2: the AI version has no long "steady base layer"

| Metric | Reference | AI-generated |
|---|---|---|
| Longest single effect, any model | 199,950 ms (~200s — nearly the whole song) | 6,900 ms |
| Typical average effect duration (non-hero models) | 1,759 – 3,208 ms | 332 – 393 ms |

The reference clearly uses at least one very long, continuous base layer (reads like a steady color-wash or
VU-meter-reactive layer spanning most of the song) underneath its shorter accent effects — this is precisely
the "hold is a steady state, never a dead one" rule already stated in this project's own prompt guidance.
The AI-generated version never does this: its longest effect anywhere is under 7 seconds. Every model is
composed entirely of short, discrete, bar/beat-cadence chunks with no long anchor layer.

Practically, this likely reads as a show that never settles — nothing to visually "lean on" between the
short accent bursts — which also compounds Finding 1 (nothing slow exists for the pace-of-change metric to
drop toward during quiet sections).

## 4. Finding 3: much shallower layering on every prop

| Metric | Reference | AI-generated |
|---|---|---|
| Max simultaneous `EffectLayer`s, richest prop | 8 (Matrixes) / 7 (Tree) | 3 (Matrixes/Outline) |
| Typical max layers, secondary props (Outline/Arches/MiniTree-class) | 3 | 0 – 2 |

Even setting aside the reference's Faces/Pictures/Video layers entirely (per this doc's own scope), its
non-media layering is still consistently deeper than the AI version's. The richest AI-generated prop uses 3
concurrent layers; several secondary props (`MiniTree2`, for example) use only 1. The reference's secondary
props routinely carry 3-4 layers even without any media content. This matches exactly the gap
`docs/research/multilayer-template-exemplars/` (already gathered, §3f/§6 of the Phase 10 plan) was collected
to close — that research has not yet been turned into enforced prompt guidance or a seed template catalog.

## 5. Finding 4: effect vocabulary is narrow and concentrated

The AI version's 6,193 effects break down into just 9 distinct names, and two effects (`Bars` and `Twinkle`)
alone account for roughly 37% of every effect in the show. The reference's 1,727 effects span 22 distinct
names with a much flatter distribution (its own two most-used effects, `Morph` and `Shockwave`, are ~25% and
~24% of the show respectively, closer to each other and to the next few effects down the list).

This reads as the Director's Show Bible settling on a small handful of "safe" Looks and the Choreographer
reusing them across most sections, rather than the wider effect palette real craft rules/exemplars (Phase 10
§3f/§2f) are meant to push toward once corpus mining actually runs — which, per Phase 10 §1a, it still does
not (`Promoted: []` is still hardcoded).

## 6. Finding 5: §3e phrase-scoped variation is wired correctly but never used, even when eligible

This run's own real section/phrase segmentation produced six sections with 2 real phrases each (confirmed
against the cached `PhraseSegmentationAnalysis` for this run) — genuinely eligible candidates for the
phrase-scoped staging Phase 10 §3e added. None of the 16 persisted `SectionScript`s for this run declared a
`PhraseVariations` entry. The feature's own compile path is independently unit/integration-tested (see
`AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN-PHASE-10-SIGNAL-UTILIZATION.md` §3e), so this is not a wiring bug — the
Choreographer is simply choosing the plain, no-staging path every time it's offered the choice. §2e's own
prompt guidance explicitly tells it staging is optional and not to manufacture it on short sections — a
2-phrase section may genuinely not warrant staging, so this alone isn't proof of a defect, but it's worth
tracking: zero adoption across 16 real calls, including on the eligible ones, suggests the optional framing
may be too easy to decline. See Phase 13 §2d below.

## 7. A process finding, not a quality finding: the configured endpoint is silently wrong

While investigating why this run took 1h14m (one single Director call alone ran ~57 minutes), a real bug was
found in request construction, independent of anything above:

`ProviderEndpointUri.Build` only knows how to strip a trailing `v1/messages` or `v1/chat/completions` suffix
before appending its own hardcoded API path. The Director/Choreographer provider is configured with Azure's
**Responses API** endpoint (`.../openai/responses?api-version=2025-04-01-preview`), which this helper does
not recognize — it silently drops the entire `/openai/responses` path *and* the `api-version` query string,
and the client ends up calling `https://<resource>.openai.azure.com/openai/v1/chat/completions` instead
(confirmed directly against the real configured endpoint string). This "worked" only because Azure happens
to expose a separate, newer OpenAI-compatible `v1` convenience surface that tolerates the classic
chat-completions shape regardless of what was configured — but it means none of the Responses-API-specific
settings (most importantly, any reasoning-effort/thinking-budget control) are actually reaching the model,
and the client has no support for the Responses API's own request/response schema at all.

This is a very plausible root cause for both the extreme per-call latency observed in this run and some of
the narrowness in Findings 2-4 above (an uncontrolled/default reasoning budget on a large prompt can produce
very different — and very slow — output than a properly bounded one). **This should be fixed and the
comparison above re-run before drawing firm conclusions about the model's own creative ceiling** — right now
we may be judging a misconfigured call, not the intended one.

## 8. Phases this report led to

Three new build-plan phases were added off the back of this report's findings, each its own full phase
document (same format as `AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN-PHASE-10-SIGNAL-UTILIZATION.md`), all slotting in after
Phase 10 in `AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN.md`'s own numbering:

- **[Phase 11 — AI Provider Request Correctness and Observability](AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN-PHASE-11-PROVIDER-RELIABILITY.md)**
  — fixes the Responses-API endpoint mismatch (§7 above), adds a reasoning-effort/thinking-budget control,
  and logs the real outgoing request so an incident like this one is diagnosable from the call log alone.
  Recommended to land first — Phase 12/13's own findings may partly reflect this misconfiguration rather than
  the model's own ceiling.
- **[Phase 12 — Section-Energy-Driven Change Density](AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN-PHASE-12-DYNAMIC-DENSITY.md)**
  — addresses Finding 1 (flat change-density) and Finding 2 (no steady base layer) with a measured per-section
  target change-rate and a new legality check comparing declared vs. measured dynamics.
- **[Phase 13 — Layering Depth and Effect-Vocabulary Diversity](AI-CHOREOGRAPHY-BUILD-PLAN-PHASE-13-LAYERING-AND-DIVERSITY.md)**
  — addresses Finding 3 (shallow layering), Finding 4 (narrow effect vocabulary), and Finding 5 (zero real
  adoption of Phase 10 §3e's phrase-scoped staging) with concrete minimum-layering guidance, a whole-song
  effect-diversity signal, and a more concrete §2e staging example.

All three are independent of each other (they touch non-overlapping prompt sections and validator rules) and
can be worked in any order, though Phase 11 should be verified first per the note above.

---

## 9. What was deliberately not evaluated here

- Faces/Pictures/Video content and timing (explicitly out of scope per this comparison's own request).
- Whether the specific Look/effect *choices* were tasteful or musically apt in a subjective sense — nothing
  above makes a qualitative creative judgment, only measures structural quantities (counts, durations,
  layer depth, density) that are objectively comparable between the two files.
- Whether `C:\xLights\The Weeknd - Blinding Lights Lite.xsq` is itself exemplary (it is one human reference,
  not a corpus-wide statistical baseline) — the comparison is useful as a concrete contrast, not as proof the
  reference is optimal.
