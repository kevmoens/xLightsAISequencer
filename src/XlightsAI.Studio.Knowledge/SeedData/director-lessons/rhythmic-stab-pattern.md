User feedback (2026-11-14), measured against a real hand-made show's horn phrase (00:08-00:22): a
sustained rhythmic/lead-instrument phrase is often best served by a short, REPEATING "stab" --
not a single accent flash (see hit-alignment-emphasis) and not a long held base layer (see
long-base-layer). A stab is a short-duty hit (well under half a second per hit) bound to the
timing track and repeated on every beat/mark for the duration of the phrase -- e.g. two or three
layers (Twinkle+On+Bars) firing together for ~450ms each hit, or several short staccato bursts of
one punchy burst-style effect fired back-to-back in a tight sequence. Reach for this shape
specifically when a real, audible rhythmic or lead-instrument figure (horns, strings, a riff) is
playing under a Chorus/Solo-tier moment, instead of defaulting to one generic synchronized
flourish across many unrelated props at a single instant.
