---
id: sweep-across-props
applies: multi-model-group-present
priority: 25
---
A literal "moving from one prop to the next" sweep (light prop A, then B, then C in turn) is NOT something a single group-targeted effect can produce -- a group effect always treats every member identically and simultaneously. Two different declared mechanisms exist for this, for two different intents -- never guess which one, pick by what you actually want: "Travel" -- a REAL sweep across a group's own individual members in true left-to-right or right-to-left physical order, each member's own moment landing exactly on a beat/transient/word so a hit lands on a specific prop on purpose: {"Order":"LeftToRight"|"RightToLeft","Timescale":"Beats"} (Timescale is the same real track vocabulary a layer's own "Timescale" uses -- "Beats", "Bars", "Phrases", "Transients", "VocalNotes"). Member 1 gets the first mark, member 2 the next mark, and so on -- this is what makes a chase across several arches or mini trees read as deliberately timed to the music instead of an arbitrary stagger. Use this for same-type prop rows (a line of arches, a row of mini trees) where the real point is "this specific one, then that one, then that one, each exactly on a beat/word." A Look declaring "Travel" applies it to every layer in that Look -- never combine "Travel" with "PropDelays" on the same Look. "PropDelays" -- a weight-based cascade, NOT aligned to the music: declare it on a Look whose Props/Subject is a group with genuinely different-sized members (mass/mid/accent tiers by real pixel weight -- e.g. {"mass":"+0ms","mid":"+80ms","accent":"+160ms"}), which staggers each tier's own reveal by a flat millisecond offset instead of firing every member at once. Use this for a graduated big-to-small (or small-to-big) cascade where landing on a specific beat/word is not the point -- reserve either mechanism for Looks where that graduated or traveling reveal is the actual intent, not every Look using a group.
